Research questions

The project's architecture exists to answer these — each one backed by a reproducible experiment under experiments/, not just a design decision.

RQ1

How does event-weighted implicit feedback (view=1, cart=3, purchase=5) affect recommendation quality versus alternative weighting schemes?

Precision@10 moves at most ~10% relative across weighting schemes tested — item-CF actually does slightly better with flatter weights.

RQ2

How does a real-time item-item recommender compare against batch ALS on quality, latency, and freshness?

CF and ALS are nearly tied on Precision@10 (0.118 vs 0.119); ALS serves 18–29x faster as a precomputed lookup.

RQ3

What is the trade-off between recommendation freshness and serving latency in a streaming + batch architecture?

The CF neighbor cache trades a bounded staleness window for O(1) lookups — live p95 fell from 90ms to 0.054ms at 5,000 items.

RQ4

Can workload-aware database optimization reduce serving latency without excessive write/indexing overhead?

The autonomous Postgres indexer cut p95 read latency 45% (6.1ms → 3.4ms) for negligible measured write overhead.

Architecture

Lambda-style: every event flows through one Kafka topic, consumed independently by a real-time (speed) path and a batch (Spark/HBase) path, with no shared database between services.

Speed layer (top): four independent Kafka consumers, at-least-once, no shared offsets. Batch layer (bottom): HDFS → MapReduce popularity job + Spark MLlib ALS → HBase, blended with live CF at serve time.

Key findings

  • CF and ALS are close on quality, ALS wins on latency. Item-CF (Precision@10 0.118) and catalog-ALS (0.119) are near-tied; ALS serves 18–29x faster since it's a precomputed lookup rather than live cosine recomputation.
  • The optimizer earns its keep, at a measured cost. The Postgres auto-indexer cut p95 read latency 45% for negligible write overhead — confirmed against real controlled traffic, not just documented.
  • Elasticsearch genuinely loses its index on container replacement — confirmed via a real container swap, not just a process kill — and only recovers once product-service, not Elasticsearch itself, restarts.
  • The naive CF lookup doesn't scale, and now it doesn't have to. A precomputed neighbor cache turned live p95 latency from 90ms to 0.054ms at 5,000 items — a 1,600–5,100x speedup depending on catalog size.
  • Kafka throughput saturates well below its own unpaced ceiling once a full downstream consumer pipeline is attached — reproduced across two independent runs three weeks apart.
